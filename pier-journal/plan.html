01 / The brief

Your journal, not another fishing feed.

Confirmed with you

  • Native Swift app for iOS 26 and later.
  • Saltwater and pier fishing in New York City and Brooklyn.
  • Log fish, save and revisit spots, see weather, and see tide conditions.
  • Private, on-device journal. No account, social features, or app-managed cloud sync.
  • Photos are most important when logging a catch: new catches open camera-first, with Choose from Photos and Skip photo alternatives, and photo review before details. Photos stay optional so quick logs are never blocked.
  • First usable milestone: phase 1a photo-first catches plus phase 1b manually saved spots. Live weather and tides follow in phase 2; export/restore follows in phase 3.
  • Two tabs: Journal and Spots. Opening a spot leads with its conditions, not a separate Conditions destination.
  • Explore Weather-style paging: Spots opens the last-viewed pier; swipe between saved spots and use All spots for the list/map.
  • Refactor the HTML around native iOS layouts and capability flows, with explicit phase labels. No native app implementation in this pass.
  • Plan and HTML wireframe before writing any native app code.
  • Keep a later AI photo-assistance idea in the plan: suggest species and useful fish information where it saves work, but use native APIs and ordinary logic for data such as location.

Proposed boundaries

Start iPhone-first, with an adaptive layout rather than a separate iPad experience. No backend, subscriptions, community spot discovery, fishing scores, automatic trip tracking, regulations database, or catch-identification AI in the first milestone. Optional photo assistance is a later exploration, not a dependency for capture or saving a catch.

Photo capture, review, optional import, and durable app-owned photos are core phase 1a work, not a later enhancement. Export/restore and richer historical analysis remain follow-ups. A saved spot records your location and notes; it is not a claim that fishing is permitted or access is open.

Local does not mean backed up. There is no cross-device sync. Device backups may include app data depending on your settings; the app cannot guarantee that. Show a plain-language warning before you rely on it as your only record.

02 / Product shape

Pick a spot. Know the conditions.

Journal
A reverse-chronological catch list. Every primary new-catch action opens capture first, with Choose from Photos and Skip photo always available. Review the candidate photo before details; Use photo accepts it into the draft, not a saved catch. Put the optional photo field at the top of details. Species and date/time remain required; date defaults to now and logging from a spot prefills that spot. Spot, length, bait, and notes remain optional. The baseline does not infer species or fabricate photo metadata; later AI suggestions require confirmation. Logging never requires a photo or creating a spot first.
Spots
Open directly to the last-viewed pier, or the first saved spot if you have not viewed one yet. Swipe horizontally between spots, like locations in Weather. Each page leads with weather and tides; scroll vertically for personal notes and linked catches. All spots opens the list/map for jumping to a place or adding a new one.
Conditions inside each spot Live data in phase 2
Weather and tides belong to the spot you opened. No separate tab, no second location picker, and no detour through a generic conditions page. Always show the spot name, tide station, timezone, and data source. Keep the native app to Journal and Spots even after live conditions arrive.

Current exploration: one pier per page. Page dots and previous/next controls make the swipe discoverable and provide alternatives to gestures. For more than three spots, use a compact position count rather than crowding the preview with dots. Paging stops at either end instead of wrapping.

One saved spot needs no paging controls. No spots opens the save-your-first-spot state. Deleting the current spot moves to its next neighbor, or the previous one at the end. Catch actions always belong to the spot where you started them.

The HTML remembers the last-viewed spot separately in Sample and First-use views, until reload. The native app should persist a validated spot ID locally. Swiping should not erase a page's vertical scroll position or interfere with reading notes.

Small, complete flows

  1. First launch → empty Journal → new catch → camera-first capture → photo review → Use photo → details → Save → see the catch in the list. Capture alone never creates a catch.
  2. Capture → Choose from Photos → select an image → review → Use photo → details; or Skip photo → details for a quick log. Denied or unavailable camera access keeps both alternatives available.
  3. Review → Retake or Choose different → discard only the candidate, not an existing draft or saved photo. Cancel a new catch with a pending photo or changed data → confirm discard or keep editing.
  4. Spots → last-viewed pier → swipe to another pier → see its conditions and catches.
  5. All spots → add a pin and name → save → open the new spot's page.
  6. All spots → choose a pier from list/map → swipe from there or scroll down for notes and catches.
  7. Spot detail → log a catch → capture first with the originating spot retained → review or skip → details with date now and spot prefilled → Save → return to that spot and see the linked catch.
  8. Catch detail → edit opens details directly, never forces camera → optionally replace or Remove the top photo field → Save. Canceling replacement preserves the old draft/saved image; only Remove explicitly clears the photo. Canceling edits preserves the saved catch. Delete requires explicit confirmation.
  9. Spot detail → choose a supported tide station if needed → see clearly attributed tides without changing the spot.

The wireframe previews phase-2 conditions inside spot details using synthetic examples. The first native milestone shows real local spot information and catches, without fake forecasts or a dead conditions panel. Live conditions will be inserted at the top of that same screen later. The map remains an illustration.

Later / Optional exploration, not implemented

Let the photo help. Keep the angler in control.

After the manual photo-first journal works, explore assistance that reduces typing and helps identify an unfamiliar fish. Start with a small species-suggestion feature, not a general-purpose chatbot. This note does not change the current wireframe or authorize model integration, photo uploads, or new dependencies.

Worth exploring

Suggest a species from the accepted photo
Offer a likely species or a short list of plausible matches, clearly labeled as suggestions. The user can accept, correct, or ignore them. If the image is unclear, contains multiple fish, or shows an unfamiliar species, ask for a clearer crop or say that identification is uncertain rather than forcing a match.
Help the user compare a match
Show relevant identifying features from a vetted species guide, such as body shape, markings, or fin characteristics. After the user confirms a species, look up its common/scientific names and concise reference information from a curated source; do not generate unsourced fish facts. A suggestion is not a verified identification.
Make the photo more useful
Optional framing, crop, or blur hints could improve the capture. Try ordinary image-quality checks or a small computer-vision model before generative AI. Never block capture, saving, or a quick photo-free log to demand a better image.

Use the right source for every field

AI is a possible assistant for ambiguous visual interpretation, not a replacement for known data.

Proposed later interaction

Capture or choose photo → Use photo → catch details → optional Suggest species action → review suggested matches → Use suggestion fills the editable species field → Save. Manual details and Save remain available while assistance runs. AI never commits a catch automatically.

Keep the raw suggestion and its source separate from user-confirmed fields. Bind analysis to the catch draft and exact photo revision; replacing/removing the photo invalidates unapplied results. A late result must not change another draft or overwrite something the user has already entered. Explain uncertain or unavailable results in plain language; do not display invented confidence percentages.

Privacy and implementation decisions stay open

Evaluate a purpose-built on-device image classifier first, for example through Vision/Core ML with a suitable trained model. Verify accuracy, image-input support, device coverage, and model size; do not assume an included Apple language model can identify fish from photographs. No model, vendor, or accuracy claim is selected here.

A cloud image service would be a separately approved exception to the on-device-only design, not a silent fallback. Explain the destination, cost, retention, and exact data sent, and get explicit consent before any upload. Minimize the request to the selected fish crop or reduced image, strip location/EXIF metadata, and exclude journal notes and saved-spot coordinates. Do not upload the library or reuse images for training by default. Provider credentials cannot be embedded in the app; any required backend is a new architecture decision.

Small experiment before a feature commitment

Once the core journal is usable, evaluate species suggestions on a consented or appropriately licensed set of NYC-area fish photos, including lookalikes, partial views, low light, multiple fish, and species outside the model's coverage. Measure corrections, false-confident results, abstentions, time/typing saved, latency, battery impact, and cost before setting a release gate. Test no-network, no-model, timeout, cancel, photo replacement, and late responses. If it is not reliably helpful, keep manual entry and skip the feature.

Sequencing: later optional experiment after the core capture/logging flow is established. It does not block phase 1, live conditions, or export/restore, and does not add AI screens to today's wireframe. Provider choice, remote processing, evaluation targets, and implementation still need explicit decisions.

Permission behavior, not a pretend system prompt

Camera and location flows are visibly marked as HTML review simulations. First camera use offers simulated Allow/Deny; after denial, future calls go directly to sticky Settings guidance plus Choose from Photos and Skip photo, never another permission prompt. Keep camera permission state separate per Sample/First-use mode, with an exterior reviewer reset for each mode. The location simulation fills a sample position on Allow and retains manual pin/coordinates on Deny. Reviewer resets are not in-app ways to bypass iOS permissions and do not open real Settings.

Native location authorization states / phase 1b
notDetermined: request When In Use only after the shortcut is tapped. authorizedWhenInUse or an existing authorizedAlways: request a location. denied: explain and optionally link to app Settings, without re-prompting. restricted: explain that access cannot be granted here and keep manual selection. Also handle system location services being off.
Accuracy and failure are separate
Reduced accuracy is not an authorization status. Accept approximate location as a starting area and let the user place the pier pin; do not silently treat it as an exact fishing spot. Handle timeout, unavailable location, and revoked permission. Add NSLocationWhenInUseUsageDescription before requesting authorization. The browser grant/deny demo does not exercise all of these OS states.
Native camera authorization and availability / phase 1a
Add NSCameraUsageDescription. On intentional camera use, .notDetermined requests access; .authorized opens still capture when available; .denied shows persistent guidance to app Settings without re-prompting; .restricted explains the limitation rather than promising Settings can fix it. Check camera availability, including Simulator and devices without a usable camera; offer PhotosPicker and Skip photo for every blocked state. Recheck authorization after returning from Settings. Never request on app launch, request microphone permission for still photos, or capture in the background.
Photo selection is not full-library access / phase 1a
PhotosPicker selection does not require full Photos-library authorization. Use it independently of camera permission. A selected image needs asynchronous loading, bounded decoding/resizing and encoding, error reporting, candidate review, and a durable app-owned copy before the catch Save commits its reference. Loading, processing, or write errors preserve the draft and any previous photo, with retry/change/skip options. Stage candidates separately from both the accepted draft photo and the saved record. 05 / Data that earns trust

Conditions, not fishing promises.

Weather: Apple WeatherKit

The native Swift API is the proposed default. Access requires Apple Developer Program membership and WeatherKit configuration for the app identifier and Xcode target. Confirm access before starting slice 2a; do not introduce a fallback provider silently.

Show the required Apple Weather mark and legal attribution link using provider-supplied attribution. If weather alerts are included later, preserve the issuing agency and required alert-detail links. The HTML reserves space for attribution but does not fetch WeatherKit data.

Tides: NOAA CO-OPS

Use product=predictions, datum=MLLW, units=english, time_zone=gmt, and format=json. Parse UTC timestamps and display them in America/New_York. Request a range covering the full NYC day plus the next event; do not treat a UTC calendar day as the NYC calendar day.

Use interval=hilo for high/low events. For harmonic stations, request interval predictions separately if a curve is needed. Subordinate stations support high/low predictions only: show an event table rather than inventing a continuous curve.

Verify a small NYC station catalog and each station's products through NOAA metadata during implementation. The Battery (8518750) is an example, not a universal station for every Brooklyn pier. Propose choices using waterway context, show station name and distance, and require a per-spot choice when suitability is unclear.

Tide height is not current speed or direction. Do not label a rising tide as a verified incoming current. Astronomical predictions do not account for all weather effects or observed water levels.

No "safe to fish" badge, legal-size judgment, guaranteed bite window, or access recommendation. This is a journal and conditions reference, not navigation or safety guidance.

Offline and partial data

Catches and saved spot metadata remain usable offline. Map tiles and live conditions require connectivity and are not promised offline. Weather and tides load independently: one failed provider must not hide the other's result.

Cached weather must show its fetched/valid times and an explicit cached or stale label; a prior forecast is not current weather. Tide predictions are labeled by the station and covered dates, and hidden outside the requested period. No cache means an honest unavailable state with retry, not sample values.

Official references

  • Apple WeatherKit: requirements and attribution
  • NOAA CO-OPS: products, datum, intervals, and timezones
  • NOAA station metadata API

06 / Acceptance criteria

Small does not mean fragile.

Every native increment

Build and run on an iOS 26+ simulator. Exercise the changed flow and its failure path. Use Dynamic Type, VoiceOver labels, meaningful focus order, 44-point targets, sufficient contrast, and no color-only status. Test light/dark mode and reduced motion. Do not ship demonstration records as the user's real data.

Journal and spots

Validate required fields, optional positive finite length, and coordinate bounds. Allow free-text species without making legal claims. Cancel must not create a record; repeated saves must not duplicate it. Dirty forms require an explicit discard decision. Entering and canceling a map, permission, or photo subflow must retain the parent draft. Relaunch preserves native edits. Permission denial does not block manual entry. Deleting a spot retains its catches and their recorded spot labels. All spots returns to the list/map, and logging from a spot retains that spot context. Test long names, empty lists, and persistence failures without losing drafts.

For paging, test horizontal swipes versus vertical scrolling, switching tabs and returning to the same spot, direct list selection, rotation/resize alignment, reduced motion, keyboard alternatives, offscreen focus, and zero/one/many spots. Deleting the active spot must select a valid neighbor, not another record's stale page index. Opening a catch form captures the spot ID before the user edits anything.

Conditions

Opening a spot must lead directly to its conditions, with no second spot selection. Use fixtures for complete, partial, empty, failed, and malformed provider responses. Test station switching, rapid navigation between spots, deleting the open spot, no saved spots, no station, cached data, timezone differences, DST transitions, and the next tide crossing midnight. Verify displayed units and source attribution. Match high/low events to provider data rather than estimating them from a decorative curve.

Photo-first catches / phase 1a

Verify every primary new-catch entry starts capture first, including from a spot; Choose from Photos and Skip photo must never block a quick log. Capture/import leads to review before details; Use photo changes only the draft. Retake/Choose different discards only the candidate. Pending/changed new catches require discard confirmation on cancellation. Existing-catch editing starts details with the photo field at the top; canceling replacement preserves the prior draft/saved image and only Remove clears it. No catch exists until explicit Save; species/date remain required, new date defaults to now, originating spot stays prefilled, and no species or metadata is fabricated.

Native tests cover .notDetermined, .authorized, .denied, .restricted, revoked permission, Settings return, and unavailable camera including Simulator; verify real still capture on a supported device. No app-launch prompt, microphone permission, or background capture. Test PhotosPicker independent of camera authorization, cancellation, failed/unsupported/oversized image loading, bounded processing, file-write and store-commit failures, retry, relaunch, and orphan cleanup. Errors retain the draft and old photo; cancel/replace/remove/delete never deletes the user's original Photos asset or a still-referenced file.

HTML acceptance is separate from native behavior: original illustration fixtures only; first-use Allow/Deny simulation; denied reuse goes straight to Settings guidance plus Photos/Skip; exterior reset and independent state per demo mode. Check capture/review/draft transitions, required validation, photo-first entry from Journal and Spots, edit cancellation, and phase labels at desktop and narrow widths, plus file:// loading. No actual camera/browser camera, location, photo-library, or file access.

Data changes and export/restore / phase 3

Never test destructive migration on the only copy of a real journal. Use a copy and a migration fixture; preserve all existing records on upgrade. Phase 3 export and restore should preserve IDs, timestamps, associations, units, and the optional app-owned photos already supported in phase 1a.

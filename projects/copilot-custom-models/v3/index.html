Copilot custom models

Helping enterprise admins choose what a model learns from, without becoming training experts.

I led design end to end, from the limited-beta refinements through the design for general availability.

~ The whole experience

The difficult questions came before training started: which data to use, and which controls an admin actually had over it.

The configuration design brings those first decisions together. Admins could select repositories, narrow the files included, and choose whether to include collected prompts and suggestions before starting training.

In June 2024 this screen went to limited beta, with refinements I made to its controls and their copy.

~ Explaining the wait

We did not have a reliable completion estimate. Instead of offering false precision, the banner at the foot of the configuration screen explained what affected training time and how people would know it was finished:

Training time is based on the amount of data selected and can take a couple of days for large data sets. We’ll notify you at {your@email.com} when training is complete.

Original design copy, email placeholder included. It describes the intended communication, not a measured training duration.

~ Two decisions, not one

Another important distinction was between permission to collect data at the organization level and the choice to include that data in an individual model. The inclusion control in the configuration screen addresses the second decision; it does not replace the organization policy.

Keeping those scopes separate mattered as much as the control itself. So did smaller details, like making the file-extension tokens consistent with Primer.

~ A starting point, not a blank slate

I explored suggested models as a way to help admins begin with a broad or more focused data set, then review it before training. Templates, repository guidance, and guided onboarding were later directions, not confirmed shipped features.

~ From a starting point to training data

A suggested starting point still needs a way to make the data choices explicit. The design for general availability took that work out of the settings card and gave it a page of its own, in two steps: choosing data sources, then specifying languages.

The repository options make different approaches visible: include everything, select manually, use recent activity, or filter by property. The language step adds feedback about coverage before continuing, bringing guidance into the decision rather than leaving it in documentation.

~ In perspective

The preview was retired before general availability, so the second design never reached anyone. What the two plates show, side by side, is the direction of travel: the same decisions moving out of a settings card and into a place of their own, with the guidance brought alongside them.

What I would carry forward is the shape of the problem. The hard part was never the training controls. It was helping an admin make a data decision they could explain to their own team, and knowing when to say what we did not yet know.
